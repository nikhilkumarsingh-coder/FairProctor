SLIDE 4 — Our solution and key differentiators  (60 s)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title: FairProctor: environment is context, behaviour is evidence
On-slide:
  1. Environment-neutral score — brightness, blur, frame rate, face visibility
     only change how much video is TRUSTED; they can never add suspicion.
     Missing video = no evidence, not suspicion. Bandwidth and device are not
     model inputs at all.
  2. Camera-free signals carry the weight — silent pauses then correct answers
     (second phone), answer timing and person-fit, rare WRONG answers shared
     across candidates (rings), leaving the window, paste size (never the text).
  3. Ranked queue with evidence — "Review first" band; every flag links to the
     moments that caused it ("Q14–Q22: 6 answers in a row in under 4 s, each
     correct, matching 3 other candidates' wrong options").
  4. Candidates see what was noticed AND what was not counted, in English or
     Hindi, and can appeal against specific moments.
  5. Phone pre-check: light, backlight, framing, sharpness, people in view,
     connection — fixes in plain language before the clock starts.
  6. Coding tests too: MOSS-style code similarity, paste and typing patterns.
  7. Fairness report per sitting: who gets flagged, by room/connection/device.
Expected outcome:
  - Reviewers start where the malpractice is: ~6% of sessions hold ~91% of cases.
  - Honest low-bandwidth candidates are not singled out (gap +3 points vs +44
    points with the old rules).
Visual: left "old: 300 flags, no reason" → right "FairProctor: score 97,
  Review first, 11 silent pauses on hard questions (00:10–23:52), head down 76%
  vs 5%". Screenshot: docs/screenshots/61-rahul-evidence.jpg.
Speaker notes:

SLIDE 5 — Architecture, workflow and algorithm  (50 s)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title: How it works
Diagram (draw as boxes and arrows):

  CANDIDATE PHONE / LAPTOP (browser, React)
    ├─ Pre-check (on device): light, backlight, face, sharpness, speed test
    ├─ Test screen: answers, timing, focus, paste LENGTH, idle pauses
    ├─ On-device face detector → NUMBERS only every ~5 s
    │    (faces, face visible, head direction, brightness) — no images leave
    └─ Offline-safe queue (IndexedDB), resend on 2G, idempotent by sequence no.
            │  HTTPS, JWT
            ▼
  FASTAPI BACKEND (CPU)
    ├─ Event store (PostgreSQL)
    ├─ Live monitor: model re-scores answers so far every 45 s → alerts
    ├─ Close & score job (whole sitting at once — needed for shared answers)
    │    1. Features + evidence per session (episodes, not frames)
    │    2. Cross-candidate answer matching (rarity-weighted, both sides, rings)
    │    3. Gradient-boosted trees (monotonic) → calibrated probability
    │    4. Environment adjustment: score − (environment-only effect)
    │    5. Explanations: contribution per family + time-stamped moments
    │    6. Bands: Review first (≥90% precision) / Review / Cleared
    ├─ Coding: sandboxed runner (Python, C++, Java, JS) + similarity + typing
    └─ Fairness report, audit log, deletion jobs
            ▼
  REVIEWER CONSOLE → ranked queue, timeline, moments, decision, appeals
  CANDIDATE RESULT → what was noticed, what was not counted, appeal

Algorithm in one line:
  score = f(behaviour, environment) − [f(honest, environment) − f(honest, typical environment)]
Visual: the box diagram above; small inset of the formula.
Speaker notes:
  "Everything heavy happens on the server on CPU; the phone sends only numbers.
  Collusion needs the whole sitting, so final scoring runs at close; a live
  monitor runs the same model on partial answers every 45 seconds. The key
  trick is the environment adjustment: we subtract what the environment alone
  would produce for an ordinary candidate, so a dark room can lower trust in
  video but can never raise the score."

FairProctor — Concept Document
Team: [Team name] · Team ID: [ID] · Members: [names] · Contact: [email, phone]
Problem statement: Proctoring that does not punish a shared room
(Integraminds · Mission Bharat · Software)

1. Problem interpretation
Remote assessments flag possible malpractice from webcam and browser rules
designed for a single candidate in a bright room on broadband. Candidates in
shared homes, cyber cafes, on phones and on 2G produce hundreds of flags that
are mostly noise, while the most common real method — a second phone below the
camera — produces none. We interpret the task as: build an integrity score that
treats the environment as context and only behaviour as evidence; combine weak
video with camera-free signals; rank the review queue by likelihood of real
malpractice with time-stamped evidence; explain the result to the candidate;
and reduce flags at the source with a phone pre-check.

2. Users, pain points, use cases, constraints
Users: candidates from small towns and rural areas; assessment reviewers;
companies and universities using the results.
Pain points: honest candidates rejected for their room; no reasons, no appeal;
reviewers overloaded; second phones undetected.
Use cases: (a) a candidate checks light and framing on a phone, then takes the
test on 2G; (b) a reviewer opens the ranked queue and confirms or clears with
evidence; (c) a candidate reads what was noticed and appeals against specific
moments; (d) an admin watches a live sitting, closes and scores it, and reads
the fairness report; (e) coding tests with code similarity and typing patterns.
Constraints: CPU at scoring time; DPDP Act (consent, purpose limitation,
deletion after retention); no face recognition beyond own-ID match; no
inference of gender, religion, caste or region; low bandwidth.

3. Existing solutions and the gap
Rule-based webcam proctoring counts frames as flags and punishes poor
environments. Deep face/gaze models need clear video and GPUs and fail at 2G
resolution (an eye is ~9 px). Live human proctoring does not scale. Code
plagiarism tools see only similarity. Gap: no separation of environment and
behaviour, little use of camera-free signals, no evidence or appeal, no
pre-check.

4. Proposed solution and differentiators
- Environment-neutral scoring: video quality only modulates trust; missing
  video is not evidence; bandwidth and device are excluded from the model.
- Camera-free signals: silent pauses followed by correct answers, timing and
  person-fit, rarity-weighted shared wrong answers and rings, focus loss before
  correct answers, paste size.
- Flags become episodes (a walk-past is one short appearance, ignored unless it
  lasts 25 s+ while answering).
- Ranked queue with bands (Review first ≥ 90% precision) and time-stamped
  evidence with jump-to moments.
- Candidate-facing explanation in English/Hindi: what was noticed, what was not
  counted, appeal.
- Phone pre-check with plain-language fixes; no image leaves the phone.
- Live monitor during the test; fairness report per sitting.
- Coding tests: sandboxed judge on hidden tests, MOSS-style similarity, paste
  and typing patterns, own trained model.
Expected outcome: reviewers see real malpractice first; honest candidates in
poor environments are not singled out; every decision is explainable.

5. Architecture and workflow
Browser (React): pre-check → consent → test; records answers, timing, focus,
idle, paste length, and on-device camera numbers every ~5 s; offline-safe queue.
Backend (FastAPI, PostgreSQL): idempotent event store; live re-scoring every
45 s; close-and-score job per sitting (features → cross-candidate matching →
gradient-boosted trees with monotonic constraints → calibration → environment
adjustment → explanations → bands); reviewer and candidate views; audit,
retention and deletion. Formula: score = f(behaviour, env) − [f(honest, env) −
f(honest, typical env)].

6. Technology stack and justification
React 19/TypeScript/Tailwind (phone-first, offline, bilingual); FastAPI (Python
end to end with ML); PostgreSQL; scikit-learn gradient boosting (CPU, ~10 s
training, 0.04 ms/session scoring, monotonic + calibrated); on-device face
detection (privacy, bandwidth); JWT + scrypt; Docker, GitHub Actions; Render,
Neon, Vercel.

7. Feasibility, risks and mitigation
Built and tested: full web app, 37 backend tests on SQLite and PostgreSQL,
6 browser suites. Risks: synthetic training data (mitigation: real pilot kit,
retrain on IMAP data); honest answer matches (rarity weighting, human review,
appeals); copying with no timing trace (known limit); 2G loss (offline queue);
privacy (consent, numbers only, deletion); code runner abuse (limits, jail for
scale).

8. Results on the labelled sample (out of fold, synthetic)
All 294 confirmed cases within the top 44.9% of sessions (55% queue cut; old
rules flag every session). Review first: 6.0% of sessions, 91.5% of cases,
90.3% precision. Precision of the first 100: 95% (old rules 47%). AUC 0.9918;
0.9915 at one frame every 5 s with recall still 100%. Honest false-flag rate:
low bandwidth 48.4%, normal 45.3% (old rules 76.4% vs 32.6%). 100% of queued
sessions have time-stamped moments. Coding model: AUC 0.989; 95% of
malpractice caught reviewing 8.9% of candidates; 3.7% honest flagged vs 79%
for a simple paste/tab rule.

9. Implementation plan and milestones
Done: engine and evaluation; checks on public datasets; web backend and
frontend with roles; live monitor; coding tests; fairness report; demo story;
Docker and CI; pilot kit. Next: real pilot with 10–20 people; deployment;
demo video. Later: map the IMAP schema and retrain; per-client thresholds;
cross-sitting ring detection; caching for live scoring.

10. Privacy (DPDP)
Consent screen; purpose limitation; numbers instead of images; paste length
not text; no sensitive inference; bandwidth/device excluded from the model;
deletion after the retention window (default 90 days) and on request; audit log.

 PART E — Q&A PREPARATION
##############################################################################
Q: Your data is synthetic. Why trust the numbers?
A: The real data wasn't available, so we simulated its shape (35% low
   bandwidth, 50% shared homes/cafes, phones held low, four malpractice
   patterns). It proves the pipeline and design choices; not exact numbers.
   We checked choices on public datasets, built a pilot kit for real people,
   and every number regenerates on the IMAP data.

Q: Why is the queue 45% of sessions?
A: The brief asks to surface EVERY confirmed case; the last few cases cost most
   of the queue. That is why we split it: Review first is 6% of sessions with
   91.5% of cases at 90% precision. Top-of-queue precision is what we optimise.

Q: How do you catch a second phone if the camera can't see it?
A: Behaviour: long silent pauses with no touch or key input on hard questions,
   no window change, then a correct answer; head-down rate in those pauses
   compared with the candidate's OWN baseline.

Q: Won't honest people who think silently be flagged?
A: One pause is normal; the pattern needs repeated pauses on hard items
   followed by correct answers, compared with the candidate's own pace, and a
   human reviewer decides. The candidate sees the moments and can explain.

Q: Doesn't using bandwidth/device make it unfair?
A: We don't use them as model inputs at all; they're recorded only for the
   fairness report. Video quality only changes how much video is trusted.

Q: What about face recognition and privacy?
A: No face recognition; face DETECTION only (count, visible, head direction)
   on the device; only numbers are sent; DPDP consent, retention and deletion.

Q: How does collusion detection work?
A: Shared answers weighted by rarity (a shared rare wrong answer counts a lot),
   same option within 20 s, strength measured from both candidates' side,
   mutual matches grouped into rings.

Q: Does it run on CPU?
A: Yes: training ~10 s, scoring 0.04 ms per session; the slow part is comparing
   answers across the sitting (~2 s per live session), which we can cache.

Q: What happens at 1 frame every 5 s?
A: AUC 0.9915 vs 0.9918 at full video; every confirmed case still above the
   line; rank correlation 0.934.

Q: Can it be gamed?
A: Monotonic model (more evidence never lowers the score); behaviour signals
   are hard to fake while answering correctly; humans make the final decision.
   Known limit: slow copying of correct answers long after the source.

Q: How is this different from plagiarism tools for coding?
A: Similarity plus behaviour: paste share, untyped characters, no corrections,
   bursts after idle, unusually fast accepts; a simple paste/tab rule flags 79%
   of honest coders, ours 3.7%.
